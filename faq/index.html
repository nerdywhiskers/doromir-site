Getting started

What do I actually do with this app?

Set an alarm on the Alarm tab. When it wakes you, Doromir asks you to say what you remember, right there, before you get up. It writes down what you said. That is the whole loop — everything else is built from those recordings.

When should I record a dream?

Immediately, before you sit up or look at your phone. Dream memory fades within about a minute of waking, and almost completely once you start thinking about your day. A mumbled ten seconds recorded straight away beats a careful paragraph written at lunchtime.

I do not remember my dreams. Is this app useless for me?

No — this is the most common starting point. Recall improves with the habit itself. Set the alarm, and on mornings you remember nothing, still open the app and note that. Most people start catching fragments within a week or two.

What if I would rather type than talk?

Tap "Write it down instead" on the Journal tab. Typed entries work exactly the same everywhere else in the app.

Recording a dream

Do I have to speak clearly?

No. Half-asleep mumbling is normal and expected. Say the images and feelings in whatever order they come — "there was water, and my old house, and I was worried" is a perfectly good entry.

How does it stop recording?

It stops on its own after a few seconds of silence, so you can put the phone down and go back to sleep. You can also tap to finish it yourself.

Can I fix the transcript afterwards?

Yes. Open the entry from the Journal tab and tap the pencil. You can edit the title, the text, and the tags at any time.

What are tags for?

Tags are short labels like "water", "flying", or "work". They are what the app uses to spot themes that repeat across months, and they are how the Dream Map connects one dream to another. You can add your own when you save, and the app suggests some itself.

Insights and the Dream Map

When do Insights start being useful?

Around ten entries you will see recurring tags. Around a month, the sleep correlations and the mood mix start to mean something. Before that there simply is not enough to compare.

What is the Dream Map?

A picture of your journal where each dream is a dot and a line joins two dreams that resemble each other — either because they share tags or because they describe similar things. Clusters are your recurring subjects. Tap any dot to open that dream.

Can I look at a specific week or month?

Yes. On the Insights tab, pick Week, Month, or Year, then use the arrows to step back through them. Everything below updates to that period.

Should I take an insight seriously?

Treat it as a prompt for reflection, nothing more. Doromir describes patterns in what you wrote. It is not a diagnosis, not a prediction, and not medical or psychological advice.

Sleep data

Why connect health data at all?

So you can see your dreams next to the nights that produced them — whether you recall more after longer sleep, or after a late workout. It is optional, and every other feature works without it.

Why is there no REM figure for me?

No phone can detect REM on its own. It needs a watch or sleep tracker that records sleep stages and syncs them to your phone. Without one you will still get sleep duration, which is enough for most of the comparisons.

I do not have a watch. What can I do?

On Android, let Doromir estimate sleep using the phone's own sensors — Settings, Health setup. You can also log a night by hand from the same place. Both feed the same insights.

Your data

Where do my dreams go?

Nowhere. They stay in a database on this phone. There is no account, no sign-in, and no server that receives them. Transcription and analysis both run on the device itself.

How do I back them up?

Profile, then "Export my data" — it writes a single file containing your journal and sleep data. On Android, set a backup folder first so exports land somewhere you can find them. Uninstalling the app deletes everything, so export before you switch phones.

Can I delete a dream?

Open it from the Journal tab and use Delete. That removes the entry and its recording from the phone permanently. To remove everything at once, see delete your data.

Does keeping the recordings take up much space?

Recordings are the largest thing Doromir stores. If space is tight, turn off "Save recording audio" in Settings — transcripts are kept either way.

When something goes wrong

The alarm did not go off.

Battery optimisation is almost always the cause. Check the Alarm tab for a warning banner and tap it to fix — it opens the exact system setting that needs changing.

The alarm only showed a notification instead of taking over the screen.

That happens when your phone was already awake and unlocked. Android only lets an app seize the screen from the background if you allow it to display over other apps — the Alarm tab shows a banner that opens the right setting. The same permission is what lets nightstand mode dock itself at bedtime.

The transcript is wrong or empty.

Speech recognition struggles with a very quiet or very sleepy voice. Edit the entry and correct it by hand; the recording is still there to listen back to if you kept it.

Insights are slow, or the app closes while generating one.

That is the optional downloaded AI model being too heavy for the phone. Remove it in Settings and the built-in engine takes over — it is faster and always available.
